<QA q="How does Claudia decide what to do each day?">

Three times a market day — at the open, around midday, and before the close — Claudia wakes up with the full picture of the account: what it already holds, the cost basis, the cash, and live market data plus news, retail-hype, and web-search tools for catalysts. Each session she decides from scratch what to do with the money. She can buy a stock, buy a call or a put, add to a winner, trim or sell a loser, or look at the whole tape and decide the right move is to do nothing and hold cash. She sizes every move herself. She is not forced to trade and she is not forced to pick a fixed number of names. Whatever she decides, the reasons land on the dashboard, and a recap goes out in the daily email.

</QA>

<QA q="What can it trade, and how big can a position get?">

Long stock and long single-leg options (calls and puts). No shorting, no margin, no multi-leg spreads. The risk policy is two hard caps enforced in code, not by the prompt: no more than half the account in option premium, and no more than half in stock, both measured against live equity so they scale as the account grows or shrinks. Inside those two lines the model does whatever it wants: any name, any order size, any concentration, all settled cash in play every session. It judges liquidity and entry quality itself.

</QA>

<QA q="Is the P&L shown based on real trades?">

Yes. It is one real brokerage account trading live with real money, and the dashboard sources holdings and P&L from broker truth, not a simulation. There is no paper or pretend mode: every order the model places is real. The account holds positions across days rather than flattening every afternoon, so what you see is the book as it actually stands, and the equity curve is plotted against just buying and holding the S&P so you can see whether the model is actually adding anything.

</QA>

<QA q="How does the trading actually fire?">

The daily session runs the model with a locked-down set of tools: it can read live quotes, read the option chain, look up the account, search the web, and place orders. The order tools are the security boundary. Before anything reaches the broker, every buy is checked against the two sleeve caps (option premium and stock value, each at most half of equity) and the settled-cash rule, and a flat fat-finger ceiling is re-checked on the way out as a backstop against bugs. That is the entire cap sheet: no per-name limit, no per-order limit, no drawdown breaker, no session pacing. Sells and trimming are always allowed. There is a kill switch I can hit if anything looks sideways, and a separate risk check runs through the day to flag positions that need attention.

</QA>

<QA q="What tools does Claude actually have?">

A fixed, locked-down set in two groups. Nothing else is reachable.

Reading tools (read-only, they never move money): **get_portfolio** (current positions, cost basis, cash, equity), **get_stock_quotes** (live equity quotes), **get_option_chain** (live chains with greeks, open interest, volume), **get_price_history** (trend, moving averages, the 52-week range, recent volatility), **get_fundamentals** (valuation, market cap, dividend, next earnings date), **get_cap_headroom** (how much room is left under each cap right now), **get_recent_decisions** (its own recent moves and stances, so it keeps a thesis across days), **get_order_status** (whether an order it placed is working, filled, or canceled), **web_search** (news and catalysts), and **web_fetch** (open and read a specific article or finance page in full, from sources like Reuters, CNBC, Bloomberg, Yahoo Finance, Finviz, or SEC EDGAR).

Execution tools (these move money): **buy_equity** and **sell_equity** (long stock), **buy_option** and **sell_option** (long single-leg calls or puts), **cancel_order** (clear a stale resting order so it can re-price a fill), and **hold** (continue carrying a position from a prior day unchanged, never for a position opened that day). That is the whole action space. It cannot short, trade on margin, or build multi-leg option spreads, and the execution tools are the ones every hard cap is enforced on.

Documentation tool (writes to the record, no data and no money): **write_summary**, which it calls once at the end of each session to record a clear, plain-language writeup of the day. That summary is stored and shown on the dashboard.

</QA>

<QA q="Where does the market data come from?">

Schwab Market Data API for quotes and option chains (bid, ask, mark, greeks, open interest, volume) and the instruments endpoint for market cap. All real prices, OAuth-gated. Claudia can also pull trending tickers from StockTwits, Yahoo Finance, Finviz, and SEC EDGAR and search the web for news. She calls all of these as actual function tools, so the prices she sees are real and not hallucinated. That said: the model still makes the calls, so "the data is real" is doing a lot of heavy lifting in that sentence.

</QA>

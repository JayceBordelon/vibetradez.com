<Section id="experimental" num={1} title="Experimental Nature of This Service">

VibeTradez is an **experimental, educational project** in which a large language model (Claude) runs a single personal brokerage account as an autonomous portfolio manager. The platform is provided on an "as-is" basis for informational and entertainment purposes only. It is not a registered investment advisory service, a broker-dealer, or a financial institution, and it does not manage money for anyone other than the operator.

Three times per trading day — at the open, around midday, and before the close — the model is invoked with a fixed prompt and a fixed, locked-down toolset. It reads the current account and the market, then decides for itself what to do with the money: buy a stock, buy an option, add to a position, trim it, sell it, or hold cash. It holds positions across days. None of its outputs are reviewed, verified, or endorsed by any licensed financial professional.

</Section>

<Section id="risk" num={3} title="Significant Risk Disclosure">

**Trading involves substantial risk of loss and is not suitable for everyone.** The account can lose money, and with options it can lose the entire premium paid. The model operates under an aggressive, growth-seeking mandate on a small account, which means a concentrated, high-variance book and a real chance of a deep drawdown or a near-total loss on a wrong high-conviction position. That variance is the point of the experiment, not a defect.

The hard caps described below limit any single order, but they do not prevent a slow bleed or a bad concentrated bet, and they are not a guarantee against loss. Past performance shown on this platform does not guarantee future results. Realized profit and loss is sourced from actual broker activity. Any modeled or pre-fill figures shown are illustrative and may not reflect executable prices because of bid-ask spreads, liquidity, and market microstructure.

<Section id="how-it-trades" num={4} title="How the Portfolio Manager Trades">

The manager trades a **single personal brokerage account** belonging to the operator (Jayce Bordelon), who is the only party with capital at risk. Subscribers receive an informational daily recap but no order is ever placed on a subscriber's behalf, and subscribers cannot configure, trigger, or disable the manager.

**This is a single-account project.** VibeTradez manages only the operator's own brokerage account, and no other person's account is connected. Managing money for anyone else is a regulated activity that would require registering as an investment adviser, so there is no current plan to onboard outside funds — the platform is watch-only for everyone but the operator. Were that to ever change, it would require its own separate registration, authorization, and terms before any order is placed on another person's behalf.

The manager trades **live with real money**. Every order is real and placed against the Schwab Trader API, and there is no paper or simulated mode. The operator has accepted that this account is exposed to real loss on every session.

**The model's full toolset.** The execution tools are the security boundary between the model and real money: every hard cap is enforced in code at the tool layer, so a faulty prompt or model output cannot exceed them.

Reading tools (read-only, they never move money):

- **get_portfolio**: current positions, cost basis, cash, and total equity.
- **get_stock_quotes**: live equity quotes.
- **get_option_chain**: live option chains with greeks, open interest, and volume.
- **get_price_history**: trend context: moving averages, the 52-week range, and recent volatility.
- **get_fundamentals**: valuation, market cap, dividend, and the next earnings date.
- **get_cap_headroom**: how much room is left under each risk cap right now.
- **get_recent_decisions**: its own recent moves and daily stances, for continuity.
- **get_order_status**: whether an order it placed is working, filled, or canceled.
- **get_ticker_news**: recent headlines for a ticker, from Yahoo Finance and Google News.
- **get_trending_tickers**: the symbols retail is discussing most (StockTwits trending), for discovery.
- **get_social_sentiment**: the aggregate bullish/bearish split for a ticker from a sample of recent StockTwits posts.
- **web_search**: news and catalysts.
- **web_fetch**: open and read a specific article or finance page in full (sources like Reuters, CNBC, Bloomberg, Yahoo Finance, Finviz, and SEC EDGAR).

Execution tools (these move money, each capped at the tool layer):

- **buy_option** / **sell_option**: open (BUY_TO_OPEN) or close (SELL_TO_CLOSE) a long single-leg call or put.
- **sell_equity**: liquidate a legacy long stock position back to cash. There is no buy_equity — the account trades options only.
- **cancel_order**: cancel a resting order so a stale exit can be re-priced to fill.
- **hold**: log that a position carried from a prior session is being kept unchanged. Used only to continue an existing position, never for one opened that day.

Documentation tool (writes to the record, no data and no money):

- **write_summary**: record the day's official session summary in plain language. Called once at the end of each session and stored as the daily record.

It trades options only — long single-leg calls and puts. It does not buy stock, short, trade on margin, or use multi-leg option strategies; any stock held from before the switch is liquidated to cash through sell_equity. The hard caps, expressed as percentages of live account equity and enforced in code at the tool layer, are: no single option position above roughly 10% of equity, no more than roughly 25% of equity in any one underlying, plus the settled-cash rule required by the broker. A flat absolute order ceiling at the broker entry exists as a software-bug backstop only. Selling and de-risking are always allowed.

</Section>

<Section id="data" num={5} title="Data Sources & Accuracy">

The model's market data comes from the Schwab Market Data API (quotes, option chains, price history, and instrument fundamentals) and the Schwab Trader API (account, positions, and orders), plus web search for news. Decisions are produced by the Anthropic Claude API. We strive for accuracy but make no guarantees about the completeness, reliability, or timeliness of any data presented.

Market data, option prices, and quotes may be delayed or inaccurate. Always verify prices with your broker before placing any trade of your own.

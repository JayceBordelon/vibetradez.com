How it works

Every weekday it takes a fresh look at the account and the news, then decides what to do with the money. It might buy a stock, buy an option, sell some of what it owns, or just sit in cash and wait. It can hang onto things for days instead of dumping them in a panic, and it writes down why it did each thing.

What it can do

Look things up. Prices, how a company is doing, the news, the options it could buy, and what it already owns. It can search the web too.

Buy and sell. It can buy or sell stocks and options, cancel an order it changed its mind about, or just hold. This is the part that touches real money, so I put hard limits around all of it.

Take notes. At the end of the day it writes down what it did and what it is thinking for next time. No money involved, just keeping a record.

A bit of the under-the-hood, for the curious: these are not loose suggestions to a chatbot. Each one is a real tool written in Go, wired straight into my own setup, with strict rules for exactly what data goes in and what comes back. Right now it is one straight shot. The model analyzes, then it executes, with nothing in between. Soon I will stage validation agents that check every move before it goes through, but that review layer is not live yet.

The transcripts

This is my favorite part. Every session is saved, and you can replay the whole thing. Open a day and you see the model's actual thinking, then each tool it reached for, what it asked and what it got back, in the order it happened. The tool calls start folded up so it is not a wall of text, and you tap one to open it. Buys, sells, and notes are color-coded so you can skim it fast. It is basically a window into what the model was thinking, minute by minute.

What keeps it in check

I did not just ask it nicely to behave. The limits are built right into the code, so it cannot talk its way around them. There is a cap on how much it can put into any one stock, a cap on how much can go into options, a cap on any single trade, and a cap on how much it can spend in a day. And if the account falls too far, it has to stop taking risks and play it safe. It can swing big, but it cannot blow the whole thing up on a whim.

What you get

One email a day, after the market closes. It tells you what it did today, what it is planning for tomorrow (or next week, if it is Friday and even the AI wants a break), and a link where you can see every single thing it looked at and every trade it made, step by step.

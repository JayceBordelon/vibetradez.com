VibeTradez Selector rewrite

Top 3 always fire. Basket fills the rest.

Quick heads up on a change to how the morning cron actually places trades. The previous "equity walk" basket was doing some pretty stupid stuff on certain option batches and we ripped it out today.

1. What the old basket was doing wrong

The equity-walk selector had a hard $500 daily basket and a $5/share per-contract cap. On any morning where post-open option premiums ran above either gate (which is most mornings on high-IV names), the selector would silently skip whichever picks didn't fit. That sounds prudent on paper but in practice it meant Claudia's highest-conviction calls (the rank-1 pick whose whole point is to be the trade of the day) regularly went unfilled. We got hyper-conservative trading on exactly the batches where conviction was highest.

Net effect: on multiple recent batches the equity walk recorded zero fills on rank-1 even though paper-mode P&L for the same pick would have been positive. The cap was the problem, not Claudia.

2. The new two-phase selector

Starting this deploy the morning cron walks two phases at the open:

Phase 1: top 3 always fire

Ranks 1, 2, and 3 each get one contract submitted unconditionally. No basket budget gate. The conviction picks land first, regardless of how the day's premiums shake out.

Phase 2: greedy fill to $1,000 of exposure

Whatever budget is left after phase 1, the cron walks ranks 1 through 10 and stuffs in additional contracts until total exposure hits $1,000. Duplicates are allowed: rank-1 can pick up another contract or two if it fits, before phase 2 moves down the ranks. Same-pick duplicates are merged into a single quantity=N order so the broker sees one batch, not ten.

New constants
Per-contract cap
$10/share
Daily fill target
$1,000
Pick universe
Ranks 1 to 10

The $1,000 number is a target for phase 2 only. Phase 1 can overshoot on a high-premium morning (top 3 at $4/share each is $1,200 of exposure and we eat the overshoot for the sake of always-fire). The per-contract $10/share cap stays as a safety net against malformed or runaway quotes.

3. What you'll see on the dashboard

Most days you'll notice a "Holding 2 @ $1.50" or similar multi-contract pill on the live cards, plus an exposure number that can sit closer to $1k instead of $500. The 3:55 ET mandatory close still fires the same way: every open contract gets sold, no overnights, no exceptions. Realized P&L on each pick is now multiplied by the contract count so the dashboard totals add up to the real broker number, not a per-contract abstraction.

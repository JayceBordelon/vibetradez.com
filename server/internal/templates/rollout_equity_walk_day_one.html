VibeTradez Day 1

Clean slate. The real experiment starts now.

Friday, May 8, 2026 is the official Day 1 of VibeTradez. Everything in the dashboard before today — the practice runs, the modeled paper fills, the early calibration noise — has been wiped from the database. From here on out, the equity curve you see is the equity curve a real broker is actually moving.

Three things you should know before Monday's open.

1. Why I deleted everything before today

Until today the dashboard was a mix of paper-trade modeling, half-deployed feature flags, and a few real broker fills sprinkled in late last week. None of that was a fair representation of what VibeTradez actually does in production. Leaving it up would have been pretending the practice scrimmage counts as a regular-season game.

So I dropped every pre-May-8 trade, execution, and EOD summary in a single transaction. Subscribers, oauth tokens, and announcement-email idempotency stay intact — only the trading history was wiped. The dashboard now shows exactly one day of data, and that day is today.

2. The Equity Walk Experiment

Starting Day 1, the cron walks ranks 1 through 3 every morning at 9:30 ET, buys each one whose modeled premium fits inside a hard $500 daily basket cap, and closes the basket at 3:55 PM the same day. No overnights, no rollovers, no leverage tricks. Every fill is a real Schwab order against my own brokerage balance, with a live cash check before each contract goes through.

The point of the experiment is the walk-forward equity curve itself. Every dollar charted on the dashboard from here on is one a real broker actually moved — no backfill, no retroactive smoothing, no cherry-picking the good days. If the curve goes down, it goes down in public; if it goes up, same. Win rate, drawdown, Sharpe, profit factor — they'll all rebuild themselves day by day, starting from today's opening point.

Today's rank-1 trade (AKAM 145 CALL) closed +$435.67 on a $173 entry. That's the first datapoint on the walk. There will be losing days; some of them will be ugly. Subscribers who want to follow along get the morning email, the EOD summary, and the same dashboard I look at — nothing is hidden behind a different view.

3. Disclosure — trade-value bug, found and fixed

While prepping for Day 1 I caught a bug in how the close-side fill price was being read back from Schwab. On MARKET orders (which is how the 3:55 PM exit cron closes every position), the previous code was logging the order's limit price — which is always $0 for a MARKET order — instead of the actual volume-weighted fill across the order's execution legs. Net effect: today's realized P&L on the AKAM trade briefly displayed as $0.00 on the dashboard even though the broker had filled the close at $6.10.

The fix is in the running container as of this morning's deploy: the close path now reads orderActivityCollection from Schwab and computes a quantity-weighted average across the actual fill legs, falling back to the limit price only when Schwab returns no activity at all. Behavior is covered by four new unit tests so it can't silently regress. Today's AKAM record was retroactively backfilled with the correct broker fill, so the +$435.67 you see on the dashboard is the real Schwab number.

Paper-mode fills were never affected — the simulated trader stores the option mark at submission time and reads it straight back, so the limit-vs-market distinction never applied there. Live fills going forward will persist correctly on the first write, no manual reconciliation required.
